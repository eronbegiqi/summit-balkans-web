## Introduction

The Peaks of the Balkans is a world-class long-distance trekking route that weaves through the remote and dramatic landscapes of Kosovo, Albania, and Montenegro.

Stretching approximately 192 kilometers, the trail traverses the heart of the wild and rugged Accursed Mountains—a region known for its steep limestone peaks, deep glacial valleys, and preserved traditional way of life.

What makes this trail unique is not only its natural beauty, but its role as a symbol of connection, linking communities that were once separated by borders into a shared experience of nature, culture, and adventure.

This is not just a hike—it is a multi-day journey through history, culture, and wilderness, where every step tells a story.

## Historical Background

### Before the Trail (Pre-2000s)

For decades, the mountainous border regions between Kosovo, Albania, and Montenegro remained isolated and difficult to access. Political tensions and strict border controls limited movement, even for local communities who shared language, traditions, and family ties.

Despite this, life in the mountains continued much as it had for centuries:
- Semi-nomadic shepherding
- Traditional agriculture
- Strong community-based hospitality

The isolation preserved the region as one of the most untouched natural environments in Europe.

### Development Phase (2009–2012)

The idea of creating a cross-border trail emerged as part of a sustainable tourism initiative supported by Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit (GIZ).

Key objectives:
- Promote peace and cooperation between neighboring countries
- Support rural economic development
- Create a sustainable tourism model
- Empower local communities

During this phase: ancient shepherd trails were mapped and connected, standardized trail markings were installed across three countries, residents were trained as guides, families converted homes into guesthouses, and a cross-border permit system was established.

### Official Launch (2012)

The trail officially opened in 2012, becoming one of the first hiking routes in Europe to allow multi-country trekking through remote border regions. It quickly gained attention for its authenticity, cultural depth, and untouched landscapes.

### Growth & Recognition (2013–Present)

Since launch, the Peaks of the Balkans has become a top destination for adventure travelers and a benchmark for cross-border cooperation through tourism. Ongoing developments include professional guiding services, improved trail marking, digital navigation tools, expanded accommodations, and increased international visibility.

## Geography & Route

- **Total Distance:** ~192 km
- **Duration:** 10–13 days (full circuit)
- **Highest Points:** Over 2,300 meters
- **Terrain:** Alpine, rocky, forested, and pastoral

### Main Starting Points

- Theth — Albania
- Valbona Valley — Albania
- Peja — Republic of Kosovo
- Plav — Montenegro

### Route Structure

The trail is circular, divided into stages connecting remote villages and valleys: Albania → Kosovo → Montenegro → back to Albania. Daily stages vary between 10–25 km. Elevation gain can exceed 1,000 meters per day. Each stage offers a distinct landscape and cultural experience.

## Iconic Peaks

- **Maja Jezercë (2,694 m)** — The dominant peak of the range, known for its imposing presence and glacial surroundings.
- **Gjeravica (2,656 m)** — A rewarding summit accessible from the trail, offering panoramic views across Kosovo and Albania.
- **Zla Kolata (2,534 m)** — A rugged and dramatic peak near the Albanian border.
- **Threeborder Peak** — A symbolic location where all three countries meet—one of the most unique spots in Europe.

## Valleys & Scenic Areas

- **Grebaja Valley** — One of the most spectacular valleys in Montenegro, surrounded by vertical limestone walls.
- **Valbona Valley** — Wide, lush, and framed by towering peaks.
- **Theth Valley** — Culturally rich with traditional architecture.
- **Dobërdol & Milishevc** — Untouched, wild remote alpine areas.

## Glacial Lakes & Water Features

The region is dotted with stunning glacial lakes:
- **Plav Lake** — The largest lake in the area
- **Hridsko Lake** — Often called the "Lake of Happiness"
- **Liqenat Lakes** — Peaceful alpine lakes near Kuqishtë
- **Gjeravica Lakes** — Three beautiful lakes surrounding Gjeravica
- **Ropojana Lake** — One of the gems of the trail, beneath the Karanfili Massif

Plus numerous unnamed high-altitude glacial lakes, crystal-clear mountain springs, and waterfalls including the famous Grunas Waterfall.

## Protected Areas

The trail passes through several national parks:
- Theth National Park
- Valbona Valley National Park
- Prokletije National Park

## Wildlife & Biodiversity

One of Europe's richest ecosystems, home to:
- Brown Bear
- Gray Wolf
- Balkan Lynx (critically endangered)
- Eagles, chamois, and diverse alpine flora

## Cultural Experience

Beyond nature, the trail offers a deep connection to local life and traditions.

Visitors experience traditional stone towers (kullas) and wooden guest houses, isolated mountain villages, and generations of families maintaining ancient lifestyles.

Hospitality is a defining feature: guests are welcomed with home-cooked meals, food is locally produced (cheese, dairy, vegetables, meat), and meals often reflect centuries-old recipes.

The region offers a rare glimpse into highland Albanian traditions, oral storytelling and folklore, and a slower, community-centered way of life.

## Cross-Border Experience

This trail is one of the few in the world where hikers can:
- Cross three international borders on foot
- Move through remote areas with no visible border infrastructure

A special border permit system allows legal and smooth passage and organized trekking experiences. Hikers often cross borders in remote mountain passes with no physical barriers, making it a truly unique experience in Europe.

## Difficulty & Requirements

**Difficulty Level:** Moderate to Challenging

- Daily distances: 10–25 km
- Elevation gain: up to 1,000+ meters
- Terrain: rocky, steep, sometimes exposed

**Requirements:**
- Good physical fitness
- Previous hiking experience recommended
- Navigation skills or certified guide
- Proper equipment (boots, layers, rain protection)

## Best Time to Visit

**Season:** June – October

- **June:** Wildflowers, fewer crowds, possible snow at high passes
- **July–August:** Peak season, stable weather
- **September:** Ideal conditions, fewer hikers, great visibility
- **October:** Quiet but colder, limited services

## Accommodation & Services

The trail is supported by a network of family-run guesthouses.

**What to Expect:**
- Simple but comfortable accommodation
- Shared rooms (private rooms sometimes available)
- Traditional meals included
- Locally sourced food

**Services Available:**
- Certified local guides
- Luggage transport (on selected routes)
- Packed lunches
- Border permit organization

## Why Choose Peaks of the Balkans

One of Europe's last truly wild mountain regions.

- A rare chance to explore untouched European wilderness
- Unique multi-country trekking experience
- Deep immersion in local culture and traditions
- Ideal for travelers seeking authentic, non-commercial destinations
- Direct contribution to sustainable local development
